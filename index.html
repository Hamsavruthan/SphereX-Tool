How it is computed (methods)
  • Time-lapse: Per pass and detector, the outlier-rejected mean (4σ-clipped mean) of all exposures aligned bilinearly to north-up, each after subtracting its own sky background.
  • Difference from all image pairs (default): Exposures of two passes are paired by smallest wavelength gap (each used at most once). Per pair D = B − s·A (s = flux scaling). The score is D divided by √(σ² + (0.2·Rmin)²), where Rmin = min(A, B) is the minimum of both passes: stationary stars are bright in A and B (residuals suppressed), moving objects are bright at the old and new position in only one pass each (Rmin ≈ 0, full sensitivity).
  • Difference from two images: The single pair of exposures with the smallest wavelength gap; D = B − A for every pixel. The values can be downloaded as CSV (unit MJy/sr).
  • Measurement per image: Background = 3σ-clipped median of a ring around the source. Position = intensity-weighted centroid in 5×5 pixels (search radius 37″). Brightness = aperture photometry in 3×3 pixels (18.5″).
  • Saturated stars: If the core pixels are saturated and flagged, the position is derived from the wings (point-symmetric mask within 7 pixels).
